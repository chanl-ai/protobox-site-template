## Twelve years on the line, then twelve years in the books

I'm Elena Torres. Before I opened a ledger for anyone else, I ran the kitchen and the front of house at a 40-seat place in Sacramento for six years, then helped a friend open her second location. Both times, the number that actually decided whether we made rent wasn't revenue — it was the gap between what we thought food cost was and what it actually was.

That gap is where most independent restaurants lose their margin, and it's almost never visible in a bookkeeping report that shows up 45 days late.

## What changed

I went back and got the accounting credentials, but I kept the kitchen habit of checking the numbers on a schedule that matches how a restaurant actually operates — weekly, not monthly, and definitely not annually. Torres Books & Margins is built around that cadence: close the week, reconcile against the POS and the vendor invoices, and put the three numbers that move margin in front of the owner before the next week starts.

## Who's on the other end of the call

It's a small practice on purpose. Every client gets me or Daniel, not a rotating junior associate learning restaurant accounting on your books. We cap the client list so that stays true.

## How to start

The first step is a 20-minute call to look at your last three months of POS reports and your current chart of accounts. No pitch deck — just enough to tell you honestly whether weekly close and margin coaching would move the needle for your restaurant, or whether you're better served by something simpler.
